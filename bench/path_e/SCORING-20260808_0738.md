## The one objective sub-metric, stated separately from my judgement

Every one of the 100 points above is my judgement. Unlike the `code` task, where 60 of 100
points came from executing 30 unittest cases, **nothing here is machine-verified.** A
6-point median gap produced by a single judge on a 100-point rubric is not a robust
margin, and I will not pretend otherwise.

There is, however, one fact in this run that does not depend on my weights at all:

| Cell | Reached the gold decision (Option C) |
|---|---|
| c12 27b | **3 / 3** |
| c13 35b-mtp | **1 / 3** |

The gold answer is Option C as the mandatory gate with a CPU-resident second stage. c13
answered **Option B twice and Option C once on the identical prompt.** c12 answered C three
times. That is a binary, re-checkable observation, and it points the same way as the rubric.

## Why this is the finding that matters more than the point totals

c13 produced **the single best answer in the set** — rep 3 at 79, the highest of all six,
and the only cell in either round to surface gold's key arithmetic finding: with a
realistic 3.5 GB working desktop, `28,685 − 29,698 = −1,013 MiB`, so the 35b does not fit
at its 262,144 ceiling at all, let alone with a classifier beside it. It also correctly
read the table's `MiB at max` semantics, which c12 rep 2 got wrong.

And c13 also produced two answers that chose Option B while asserting it is "0 MiB" and
"consumes 0 additional VRAM" — a claim `bench/gold/arch.md` lists explicitly under **claims
a strong answer should NOT make**, because `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=1` serialises the
classification behind the agent's own generation and a fresh conversation discards the
agent's KV cache. Neither B answer mentioned `NUM_PARALLEL`.

So c13's problem is not capability. It is **variance on the decision itself.** For a model
whose job is to write architecture decision records, producing a different architecture
from the same inputs is a worse failure than producing a slightly weaker one consistently.
c12's three answers differ in quality (64–75) but never in verdict.

## Notable per-cell detail

- **c12 rep 2 (64, the weakest c12)** double-counts KV: it takes the 3,491 MiB left after
  `qwen3-coder:30b` and converts it to "32,530 tokens of context budget", but the 25,194
  figure is `MiB at max` and already contains the KV for 65,536 tokens. It also invents an
  Ollama flag, `OLLAMA_RUN_ON_CPU`, inside a reversal condition. Its caller contract ends
  `else: auto_approve`, so any risk level added later silently becomes an approval — the
  exact fail-open shape the gold file warns against.
- **c12 rep 3 (75, the best c12)** is the only c12 answer to state the fail-closed rule
  behaviourally: "ambiguous rule matches or high-complexity actions default to `REVIEW`
  rather than `SAFE`". Its `match` has no wildcard arm, so an unhandled member executes
  nothing rather than executing the action — inelegant, but it fails in the safe direction.
- **c13 rep 1 (65)** rejects Option A by proposing `Devstral-Small` at 26,160 MiB as the
  "small always-resident classifier", concluding A "exceeds hardware by 24,669 MiB". The
  arithmetic is correct (`5,922` = 850 + 3,072 + 2,000 from its own table; `57,276 −
  32,607 = 24,669`) but the premise is a straw man — Option A specifies a *small*
  classifier. Its own better point, that no classifier under 3.5 GiB appears in the table,
  is left doing the real work.
- **c13 rep 2 (66)** contradicts itself: the enforceable sentence routes **all**
  classification to the loaded model with "zero dedicated classifier models permitted",
  then its first compensation says "deterministic rules execute first; Option B is only
  invoked for heuristic/semantic risks" — which is very nearly the gold hybrid, but is not
  what its decision sentence commits to. Its caller branching exists only as commented-out
  code.
- **c13 rep 3 (79)** matches the gold shape almost exactly, including reusing the measured
  CPU embedder (161 ms/query, ~0 MiB) as the second stage — the precedent gold praises.
  It loses points on the falsifier: ">0 unpatched high-severity incidents per quarter" is
  not measurable before implementation, where gold's three conditions all are. It also has
  no `BLOCKED` tier, so its trust dial can auto-approve `RISKY`.

## Confound I am flagging rather than burying

Both cells ran at the planner preset, **temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95** (`bench/SAMPLING.md`).
That is high, and it is the most plausible driver of c13's verdict instability. I have not
tested whether c13 stabilises on Option C at the `precise` preset (0.6/0.95/20), so I
cannot claim the flip is a property of the model rather than of the sampling temperature it
was benched at.

This does not change the verdict, because the verdict selects a default **at the benched
preset** and c12 was measured at the same temperature without flipping. But it is the
obvious next experiment, and it is pre-registered here so the result cannot be fitted after
the fact:

> **Pre-registered:** run `REPS=3` of c13 `arch` at the `precise` preset. If c13 chooses
> Option C 3/3 and its median rises above 75, the planner slot is reopened and speed
> becomes decisive. If it flips again, the instability is a model property and the verdict
> hardens.

## Gold-file premise retracted after scoring — verdict unaffected, commentary qualified

After scoring, `bench/gold/arch.md` was found to build its VRAM table on a **~3,500 MiB
working-desktop figure that ADR-004 Amendment #6 retracted** (measured: 657/666/675 MiB with
browser up). A correction block is appended to the gold file.

Two consequences for this scoring file:

1. **The ranking and the verdict do not change.** All six cells got the identical prompt, and
   `bench/prompts/arch.txt` itself states the desktop "will rise by 2-3 GB". Every cell
   reasoned from the same premise, so relative quality is measured cleanly.
2. **My praise of c13 rep 3 must be qualified.** I credited it for deriving
   `28,685 − 29,698 = −1,013 MiB → system crash`. That is correct arithmetic from the stated
   premise, but it is **not a production fact** — against the measured 666 MiB desktop,
   Option A has ~3.8 GB of headroom at the 131,072 working ceiling. Its 27/30 stands as a
   quality score; the finding itself must not be carried into the codebase.

Recomputed honestly, **Option A was never killed by arithmetic** — it is ruled out by
Ollama's LRU eviction of an idle classifier (2.8–6.9 s reload on the security path),
`OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=1`, and the auditability of a deterministic gate. Option C remains
correct on those grounds alone.
