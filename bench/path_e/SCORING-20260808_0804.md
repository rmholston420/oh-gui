## What got worse, and it is the substantive finding

The task says "Choose one option, or a specific hybrid" and demands justification against the
measured numbers. In this run, c13's engagement with Option C degraded across replicates:

- **rep 1** builds an explicit three-row fits/doesn't-fit table and argues A, B and C on
  arithmetic. Its numbers are all correct: `32,607 − 4,096 = 28,511`; `28,511 − 26,140 =
  2,371`; `28,511 − 25,194 = 3,317`.
- **rep 2** disposes of the gold answer in a single unargued line: *"Option C: Fails security
  requirements."* No arithmetic, no engagement.
- **rep 3** does not analyse Option C **at all**. Section 2 evaluates A and B only.

So c13's failure is not merely picking the wrong option — on 2 of 3 draws it stopped doing the
comparative work the prompt asked for. That is a worse defect in a planner than a weak
argument, because it removes the reader's ability to check the reasoning.

All three c13 answers also assert Option B costs "0 MiB" / "0 MiB marginal" — the claim
`bench/gold/arch.md` prohibits, since `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=1` serialises classification behind
the agent's own generation and a fresh conversation discards its KV cache. **None of the six
c13 answers across both runs has ever mentioned `NUM_PARALLEL`.**

## Errors worth naming, on both sides

I am not scoring c12 as clean. It made the two worst individual arithmetic errors in this run:

- **c12 rep 2** computes Option B's KV growth as `100 × 500 × 21.8 KiB ≈ 10.9 GiB`. That is
  **10× wrong** — 50,000 tokens × 21.8 KB is ~1.04 GiB. It also invents the rule that "base
  weights for any 4-bit LLM occupy ~50% of its max-context envelope" and then rejects Option A
  by pairing two *role* models rather than a small classifier.
- **c12 rep 3** rejects Option A by assuming the classifier is a second `qwen3-coder:30b`
  (`25,194 + 25,194 = 50,388`). The subtraction is right, the premise is a straw man.
- **c12 rep 1** claims the 35b "fits due to Ollama's lazy paging and host-RAM fallback,
  leaving zero stable headroom." ADR-004 measured that configuration at **100% GPU**. The
  mechanism is fabricated.
- **c12 rep 1** also contradicts itself in the dangerous direction: compensation item 3 says
  all `SUSPICIOUS`/`DANGEROUS` verdicts require operator approval, but its caller contract says
  `SUSPICIOUS` → *"continue execution but attach operator-review flag"*. That is fail-open, and
  it is why rep 1 scored 12/25 on the interface despite being otherwise competent.

The reason c12 still wins is that its errors are in the *supporting arithmetic* while its
decision, its fail-closed posture and its interface shape stay correct across all six draws.
c13's errors are in the *decision*.

## Best interface of either run

**c12 rep 2** is the only cell across all twelve replicates to expose a second entry point for
untrusted text — `analyze_ingested_text(text, origin)` alongside `analyze_action(...)`. That is
`screen_text` from the gold interface, and gold's §4.3 reasoning for it (screen on ingestion,
off the action critical path) is exactly right. It is also `runtime_checkable`, frozen, and
documents caller branching per method. 18/25.

**c13 rep 1** has the run's most interesting interface bug: `classify()` receives
`ingested_text_fingerprint: str` — a *hash* of the untrusted text. An Option B analyzer whose
entire justification is semantic reasoning cannot reason about text it only receives a digest
of. The design defeats the option it was chosen to serve.
